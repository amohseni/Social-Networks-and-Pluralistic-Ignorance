# Model specification of 2026-09-11, amended 2026-09-16 (supersedes the 2026-09-09 design's base game)

Source: Aydin's instruction of 2026-09-11. This is the model the R engine (`R/engine.R`) implements. The 2026-09-09 design doc in for-claude and its Python engine describe the earlier Truth-and-Conformity payoff with credences; they are historical.

## The model

A population of n agents on an undirected network. For each agent i:

- a private attitude A_i in {0,1};
- a public declaration D_i in {0,1};
- a conformity parameter c_i in [0,1], drawn i.i.d. from F_c (uniform, beta(a,b), or a point mass), fixed for the run.

Payoff for declaring D: U_i(D) = c_i N_i(D) + (1 - c_i) 1[D = A_i], where N_i(D) is the share of i's neighbors declaring D.

Dynamics: agents best-respond one at a time in a random order each round until a full round passes with no change in declarations (cap 500 rounds; non-convergence is recorded). Attitudes are fixed during play.

Two points the instruction left implicit, resolved as follows (both easy to change):

1. The truth term is written (1 - c_i) A_i in the instruction; a term that does not depend on D cannot affect the choice, so it is implemented as the indicator that the declaration matches the attitude. Declaring A_i then beats declaring 1 - A_i by (1 - c_i) - c_i (1 - 2 N_i(A_i)).
2. Ties (exact equality) go to the private attitude. Ties have measure zero under continuous F_c and matter only for point-mass types.

Consequences worth knowing: c_i = 0 is always sincere; c_i = 1 follows the local majority; an agent flips against its attitude iff c_i > 1 / (2 - 2 N_i(A_i)), so with no local support it flips iff c_i > 1/2.

## Definitions

- Perceived prevalence: agent i's estimate of the population share holding attitude 1, taken to be the share of i's neighbors declaring 1 (face value).
- Pluralistic ignorance (amended 2026-09-16, neighbor-relative) is a state of an individual agent judged against its own neighbors: the agent believes that a majority of its neighbors hold the attitude opposite to its own (perceived share of neighbors sharing its attitude below 1/2); that belief is mistaken (true share sharing its attitude at least 1/2); and it declares the opposite attitude. Holders of either attitude can be in the state. The prevalence is the share of all agents in the state (`pi_prevalence`), also reported per attitude (`pi_prevalence_a1`, `pi_prevalence_a0`). The pattern used as the acceptance criterion for corpus datasets is the first condition alone for more than half of the agents (`apparent_share` > 1/2, `pattern_present`), and the same for respondents. Tests of pluralistic ignorance estimate the prevalence. The perception-gap decomposition is still computed and stored in the key but is no longer part of the exposition.
- Perception: agents take each neighbor to hold the attitude it declares, except under clairvoyant perception (`perception = "attitudes"`), where agents observe neighbors' true attitudes, conform to those, and the survey reports true contact attitudes.
- The alternative explanation: the friendship paradox coupled with a positive correlation between centrality and the minority attitude. The two-term decomposition E[ghat] - p = E[fhat - f] + Cov(A, r) (misperception + structure) is unchanged from the identifiability note.

## Scenarios (display names in the GUI)

- S1 "Conformity from a random start": attitudes i.i.d. with prevalence pi_0, declarations random (or sincere); run to a fixed point.
- S2 "Well-connected minority": floor((1 - pi_0) n) agents receive attitude 0, sampled without replacement with weight centrality^lambda; centrality is degree, eigenvector, or betweenness (igraph). Three versions (amended 2026-09-16): `pure = TRUE`, conformity off (c_i = 0), sincere declarations, accurate perception, prevalence of pluralistic ignorance exactly 0; `pure = FALSE, perception = "declarations"`, conformity to declarations as in the other scenarios, both decomposition terms nonzero, prevalence possibly positive; `pure = FALSE, perception = "attitudes"`, conformity to true attitudes (clairvoyant), declarations differ from attitudes but perception is accurate, prevalence exactly 0.
- S3 "Private change of mind": all hold and declare 1; a fraction psi flips attitude to 0; run to a fixed point. Under uniform c_i the old declarations tip; under beta(3,1) they persist (pluralistic ignorance).

Default network: preferential attachment (Barabasi-Albert, m = 2).

## Exported data (survey designs, replacing the tier vocabulary)

`respondents.csv` always has respondent_id, attitude, perceived_prevalence; recording degree and declaration adds those columns. `ego_network.csv` (one row per respondent-contact pair, contacts anonymous and shuffled) exists when contact perceptions are recorded, with perceived_neighbor_attitude, optionally neighbor_degree, and under the linked design neighbor_id and neighbor_attitude. Cross-reference to the identifiability note: T0 = attitude and perceived prevalence; T1 + degree; T2 + declaration; T3 + anonymous contact perceptions; T3' + contacts' degrees; T4 linked.
